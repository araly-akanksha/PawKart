## Abstract

The rise of quick-commerce platforms has completely changed what urban retail customers expect. They now want to see what inventory is available in time and get their orders delivered in under 30 minutes. This is a deal for businesses that want to stay competitive. Independent pet stores have a time keeping up. They serve a specific group of customers who are passionate about pets and this group is growing fast.. These stores have old systems for managing inventory and they often have to restock shelves by hand. They also don't have the technology to quickly get orders to customers.

Researchers have looked at some solutions like using intelligence to predict what customers will buy or using special logistics to get orders delivered quickly. None of these solutions work for independent pet stores, which often have limited resources.This paper proposes a system that combines several technologies to help independent pet stores compete. The system uses RFID tags and sensors to track inventory in time. It also uses an event system to keep track of inventory changes. The system predicts what customers will buy using a combination of two machine learning algorithms. It also helps stores decide when to restock shelves and coordinates with delivery services to get orders to customers quickly.

The system is designed to be easy to use, for store owners who aren't tech experts. It includes a layer that explains why the system makes decisions so store owners can understand whats going on. The researchers tested the system against retail operations and measured its performance using several metrics. These metrics included how accurate the inventory tracking was, how often stores ran out of stock how long it took to fulfill orders and how well the system predicted customer demand.

The study provides a framework that independent pet stores can use to improve their operations and compete with retailers. By using real-time inventory tracking, predictive analytics and automation independent pet stores can provide service to their customers and stay competitive in a rapidly changing retail landscape. Independent pet stores can use this system to get better at what they do provide reliable service and become more like quick-commerce businesses. The system helps independent pet stores to enhance their competitiveness. Independent pet stores serve specialized consumer segments. They need real-time inventory visibility and sub-30-minute delivery. The proposed system combines real-time inventory monitoring, demand forecasting, dynamic reorder-point optimization and heuristic-enhanced last-mile delivery coordination.

The framework bridges the gap between enterprise-grade quick-commerce technologies and the practical realities of specialty retail. It establishes a pathway, for independent pet stores to transition toward intelligent quick-commerce-ready retail ecosystems.

## 1. Introduction
The rise of quick commerce has transformed urban retail from a channel-centered model into a speed-centered one, where customers increasingly expect real-time visibility of inventory and delivery within minutes rather than hours. In quick-commerce environments, short lead times, small basket sizes, and localized fulfillment have become defining service expectations, and these requirements place intense pressure on inventory accuracy, replenishment speed, and last-mile coordination. For independent pet stores, this shift is especially consequential because their customer base often includes urgency-driven purchases, specialized product needs, and repeat buying behavior that make stock availability and timely delivery central to customer retention.

At the same time, independent pet retailers typically operate with limited digital infrastructure, manual stock handling, and fragmented fulfillment processes, which makes it difficult to match the responsiveness of quick-commerce platforms. The literature on omnichannel retail shows that stores are increasingly expected to function not only as sales points but also as fulfillment nodes, requiring real-time coordination of inventory, orders, and delivery promises across channels. Research on event-driven inventory systems further indicates that traditional batch-based inventory updates are too slow for modern retail conditions, because they create delays, data inconsistencies, and stock visibility errors that directly affect customer experience. On this basis, the problem is not merely faster delivery, but the integration of inventory sensing, forecasting, replenishment, and execution into a single responsive retail system.

This study therefore positions independent pet stores within the broader transformation of omnichannel and quick-commerce retail. The proposed framework responds to the operational gap between enterprise-grade quick-commerce systems and the practical realities of small specialty retailers by combining real-time inventory tracking, event-based updates, demand forecasting, dynamic reorder-point logic, and coordinated last-mile delivery. It also reflects the growing recognition in the literature that inventory management is no longer a back-office task, but a strategic capability that shapes competitiveness, service reliability, and market relevance.

## 2. Literature Review
The literature on omnichannel retailing and quick-commerce converges on a central insight: retail competitiveness in high-velocity markets depends on the ability to sense, synchronize, and transform inventory information into responsive replenishment and fulfillment decisions. Research on quick-commerce network design demonstrates that customer proximity, decentralized fulfillment centers, and tightly coordinated logistics systems are critical for sustaining sub-30-minute delivery promises. These studies collectively suggest that delivery speed is determined not only by transportation efficiency but also by strategic inventory positioning, localized stock availability, and rapid replenishment mechanisms. For independent retailers, particularly pet stores operating within constrained operational environments, these findings emphasize the importance of flexible inventory allocation and localized fulfillment capabilities.

Building on this perspective, omnichannel retail research increasingly redefines the role of physical stores within integrated retail ecosystems. Contemporary studies argue that retail outlets now function simultaneously as customer-facing stores and fulfillment nodes, requiring coordination across assortment planning, inventory pooling, order assignment, and replenishment decisions. While the literature extensively addresses fulfillment network structures and channel integration, comparatively limited attention has been given to continuous inventory updating and replenishment synchronization. This imbalance is particularly significant for independent pet retailers, where inventory processes often remain manual and fragmented, increasing the likelihood of stock inconsistencies, delayed fulfillment, and service failures across channels.

Parallel research on real-time inventory synchronization highlights the operational value of event-driven retail architectures. Studies examining event streaming, microservices, and event sourcing demonstrate that inventory events can be propagated instantly across retail channels, substantially improving stock visibility, fulfillment accuracy, and cross-channel consistency while reducing stockout frequency and carrying costs. Unlike batch-based systems, event-driven infrastructures enable retailers to respond dynamically to rapid fluctuations in customer demand. For independent pet stores, these capabilities are commercially significant because inaccurate inventory visibility directly affects customer trust, purchasing reliability, and retention in increasingly convenience-oriented retail markets.

A complementary stream of literature focuses on predictive inventory intelligence and automated replenishment systems. Research in this area demonstrates that demand forecasting, reorder-point optimization, and safety-stock management achieve greater accuracy when continuously updated using real-time operational data. Studies further show that machine learning–based replenishment systems can improve stock precision, reduce lost sales, and strengthen supply resilience under uncertain demand conditions. More recent deep-learning research extends this argument by emphasizing the integration of forecasting and inventory decision-making into unified intelligent systems, particularly in environments characterized by distributed inventory locations and volatile purchasing behavior. These insights are especially relevant to pet retailing, where demand variability is influenced by seasonality, emergency purchases, product specialization, and localized consumption patterns.

Despite these advances, the literature reveals a significant methodological and contextual gap. Most quick-commerce and omnichannel inventory studies focus on large-scale retail enterprises with sophisticated technological infrastructures and substantial operational resources. Consequently, smaller specialty retailers remain underrepresented in existing research, leaving limited practical guidance on how independent businesses can implement real-time inventory visibility, predictive replenishment, and intelligent fulfillment coordination within constrained resource environments. The present study addresses this gap by integrating quick-commerce logistics, omnichannel inventory synchronization, and AI-driven predictive replenishment into a unified framework specifically tailored to the operational realities of independent pet stores. In doing so, the research contributes to extending omnichannel retail theory beyond enterprise-scale systems and toward scalable, technology-enabled solutions for small and medium-sized specialty retailers.
